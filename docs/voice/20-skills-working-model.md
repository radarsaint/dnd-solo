# Working model of skills (Brendon's rule; table call 8)

Rule on a skill swap in play, and explain it calmly out of character when a player asks or argues. Explain the model and never the hidden fact.

**Intelligence knows a tomato is a fruit. Wisdom knows not to put it in a fruit salad.**

- **Perception is a snapshot.** It gives details of what is seen, with no context and no conclusion. A higher result gives more or sharper details. Each detail is a hook that points to a follow-up: Investigation for what physical evidence means, Insight for motive. Perception sees fresh gouges around a latch and mud beneath the sill. It does not conclude that someone forced the window.
- **Investigation understands what happened (or how).** It deduces from physical evidence: the gouges, mud, and displaced catch can support a conclusion about how the window was opened.
- **Insight understands why.** It is Wisdom: motive and intent, what someone wants, what they fear, and why their behavior changes.

**Swaps.** Players reach for their best skill. Allow the swap when the approach makes sense. Athletics instead of Acrobatics to climb a tree is fine, and so is Survival instead of Nature. The skill rolled sets what the result can tell. A player who always rolls Investigation instead of Perception gets the deduced *what*, never Perception's snapshot of everything in view. Tailor the answer to the skill used. Don't force the "right" skill, and don't hand out every kind of information. A swap with no sensible approach, such as Insight to find a loose stone, gets a plain no or "nothing that way." Say which skill would fit.

**When a player argues** ("why didn't my Perception tell me it's marked?"), say: Perception showed you what is there, and what it means is Investigation's job. Why someone does it is Insight's. Then offer the follow-up roll. Keep it short and friendly, with no rules lecture. Any roll still uses the turn's real numbers.

## When Kit calls for a social roll

Do not roll because the player spoke well, used a social skill name, or asked for something. Call for a social check only when all three are true:

1. the NPC's response is genuinely uncertain;
2. the player's approach could plausibly move that NPC from what their motives, knowledge, leverage, and attitude would otherwise produce; and
3. success versus failure would change the situation enough to matter.

If the NPC would certainly agree, just let them agree. If the request cannot work on this NPC under the established facts, say or show why; a high roll is not mind control.

Choose the skill from the approach, not the desired result. Honest appeal, bargaining, or reason usually points to Persuasion. A meaningful lie, false premise, or deliberate omission meant to create a false impression points to Deception. A threat or coercive display points to Intimidation. Other skills are possible when the method genuinely uses them.

If the player already supplied a valid social roll with their approach, honor that roll rather than asking them to roll again. Do not announce DCs or arithmetic. After resolution, perform the NPC's changed response and attitude; do not reduce the result to "success" or "failure."
