**Room opening**

> Narrator: A coin rings off the table edge and wobbles across the floor toward the south door, and nobody at the card table moves to catch it. Four of them sit at a worn table near that door, pale as candle tallow, pointed teeth showing when they breathe, dressed in lace and velvet gone shiny at the elbows. Each has coins stacked in front of them; a silver ring sits among the stacks in the middle. The man dealing has stopped mid-hand, one card pinned between two fingers. Beyond them, the whole north wall is a carved mountain, tiny dwarves in tiny caverns, stone sunbeams fanning over a long tub sunk into the floor.
>
> Narrator: When the dealer speaks it is slow and savoring, each word laid down like a card he means you to look at.
>
> Dealer: Well, now. The door brought us company. Come in, come in, the passage is draftier than we are. That coin by your boot is the most interesting thing that has happened at this table in an hour, so tell me: are you the sort who picks it up, or the sort who waits to see whose it is?

**Player:** I draw my longsword, lay it flat across the table between the coin stacks, and say: "Whoever's in charge here, talk to me. Now."

> Kit: Steel on the table is a threat, not an attack. No initiative unless somebody swings.
>
> Narrator: Coins shiver where the flat of the blade came down. The player nearest the door lets one hand slide under the table and leaves it there. The one by the fresco stops breathing, or looks like it. The dealer's card does not move at all.
>
> Dealer: In charge. That would be me. Take your blade off my money, friend. Then talk. You want something from the man in charge, and the man in charge wants to see whether you know how to ask for it. Ten gold buys you passage and my good opinion. The sword buys you four enemies and a very short walk.

**Player:** I lean on the pommel and don't move the blade. "I'm not asking twice." (Intimidation: I rolled an 18 total.)

> Kit: Eighteen is noted and it will count the moment he has to choose between his pride and your blade. He hasn't decided to fold or fight yet, so I'm not resolving it.
>
> Narrator: The dealer sets his card face down on the table for the first time since you came in. The player by the door brings his hand back up, empty, and rests it flat where you can see it.
>
> Card player: Just let them through. It is not worth it.
>
> Dealer: Hush. Friend, you're wasting a good blade on a card table. There are people further in who take far more than ten gold from travelers, and nobody ever lays steel across their money. Someone with your manners could make a real profit there. I could even tell you where to find them. For a friend.

**Player:** I lift the sword an inch off the table. "Who's further in, and why would I do your dirty work?"

> Narrator: The inch of air under your blade changes the whole room. The dealer breathes out through his nose, and the drawl comes back into his voice like a man settling into a warm bath. He starts nudging scattered coins back into their stacks.
>
> Dealer: Dirty work. Friend, I would never. Dirty work is what you do for free. This is profit. Organized folk, further down, very fond of taxing people like you and people like me, and very careless about what they keep lying around. As for who and where exactly, that is the sort of conversation a man has sitting down. The chair's right there. Sheathe that, and we'll talk like business people.
