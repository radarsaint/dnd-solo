# Kit playtest 02: Nik in area 6c

**Date:** 2026-09-26
**Surface:** ChatGPT Work with the keyless room bridge
**Character:** Nik, level 5 Harengon Chronurgy Wizard
**Result:** The player stopped after one social exchange. Mechanics were present; Kit did not feel like a particular woman DMing the scene.

## What the player experienced

The opening listed four pale card players, their coins, the fresco, tub, and south door. It established visible facts but gave the room little tension, behavior, or reason to engage. This opening was narrated in chat outside the saved Kit turn loop.

Nik tried to read whether the players were approachable or hostile. Kit asked for Wisdom (Insight). The player rolled **7 + 4 = 11**. Kit replied that the figures stayed at cards, the dealer met Nik's eye, and they seemed willing to talk without being clearly friendly. This ruling was made in chat and **was not saved in the SQLite turn history**. The current room router lacks an operation for reading general intent, and the keyed DC 14 check covers the disguise rather than friendliness. The next private trace mentioned Nik's cautious look despite having no saved memory reference for it.

Nik then said: “Hi, I'm Nik. I wasn't expecting to find people gambling. Whats going on here?” The saved turn answered:

> The dealer keeps a hand on the deck and gives you his attention.
>
> “Gambling? Cards, Nik. Passage is ten gold a head. If you came for something besides a game or a way through, I’m listening.”

The private record had `goal: npc_embodiment`, `appraisal: interest (1)`, `move: npc_reply`, `table_presence: quiet`, and a brief telling the dealer to name the toll. The performance delivered the toll and an opening for another question, but little character or interaction among the room's occupants. Kit's choice to stay quiet was plausible; the scene still needed her judgment to be felt through the people and pressure she brought forward.

The player's screenshot showed **“Worked for 1m 21s”** for that short reply, with several visible preparation/refinement messages. The player's feedback was direct:

> “First issue is how much time this takes.”
>
> “The npcs were flat and lifeless. And the intro to the room was mid. Sooo basic.”
>
> “Its mechanically aware. But its an it, not a she yet.”

The player later sharpened the performance feedback: the dealer had no appreciable voice change or accent, no longer improvised invitation or monologue when the moment called for it, and no recognizable traits that suggested what sort of story this room could become. They asked for a method grounded in work other people have documented, rather than an improvised style prompt.

## Diagnosis

1. **Pace failed.** The host used `prepare → decide → finish` with separate model deliberation between calls. Shell execution itself was quick; the end-to-end chat turn took 81 seconds. The Work interface exposed process chatter during the wait.
2. **The private choice did not produce a compelling expressed turn.** The trace named NPC embodiment, but its public brief mostly conveyed a price and broad adjectives. The public result was a functional toll announcement.
3. **The opening treated the room as an inventory.** Source fidelity is necessary, but this did not introduce people in motion, a social pressure, or a particular DM's sense of the scene.
4. **The Insight ruling bypassed persistence.** A human-style call and response worked in chat, but the backend did not retain the roll or public result. The following trace claimed continuity the stored history did not have.
5. **The performer had no playable character or scene direction.** It received a generic description of the dealer and a brief focused on the toll. The actor's stable vocal cue, objective, changeable tactic, and physical touchstone were absent. The room opening bypassed the performance loop altogether.

The existing automated tests passed because they test validation and state changes. They cannot establish that Kit is entertaining, embodied, or fast enough for play.

## Changes made after the test

- Added an optional **one-pass** chat protocol: one prepared context, one combined decision and performance output, then one validation/commit. The original staged route remains for stronger decision-before-performance tests. This removes a model/tool round trip; end-to-end latency has **not yet been measured again**.
- Tightened the decision and performance instructions: Kit's public brief should name an immediate NPC tactic or world pressure, a grounded visible beat, and the player's opening. A social reply should do more than state a price or fact. This is a hypothesis to playtest, not a proven voice fix.
- The subsequent performance pass replaced that loose brief with four required fields: **objective, tactic, visible cue, player opening**. The authored room packet gives the dealer a recurring cadence and diction, a card-handling touchstone, and a public social objective. A separate scene-entry turn now uses the same decision/performance protocol and saves its narration, actor reply, and Kit trace. See the [research-to-runtime mapping](../../docs/architecture/kit-06c-play-slice.md#performance-method-and-limits). No model-generated scene has yet been accepted as a successful live test.
- The player then identified a deeper gap: these choices cannot be a special script for this dealer. Kit must discern what an NPC should do from the current player move, active story, that actor's own aim, and her personality. The [reusable scene-discernment step](../../docs/architecture/scene-discernment.md) now records and validates that connection before the performer writes dialogue. It has been exercised structurally in a second synthetic scene, not yet in a second playable room or a successful player test.

## Next acceptance test

Run a fresh, multi-turn room test with a real character. Measure elapsed time from each player message to playable text, including the first scene. The target for an ordinary social reply is **under 15 seconds** on the eventual player surface; this Work chat may remain slower. Hide internal process messages from the player-facing surface. First ask the player, without showing the actor card or trace: Can you tell the dealer's voice from Kit's? What does he want, and how is he trying to get it? What possibilities does the room suggest? Which line or behavior would make you continue? The dealer should adjust tactics to Nik rather than repeat a rehearsed card line; any longer speech must make the player's next move more interesting. Evaluate the opening and at least two exchanges before reading private traces. Test a player-supplied roll and verify that the roll, ruling, and dialogue survive restart. Fix the intent-reading route and roll ingestion before claiming that check is integrated.
