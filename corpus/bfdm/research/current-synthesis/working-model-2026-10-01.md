# Working Synthesis — 2026-10-01

**Status:** provisional research notes.  
**Authority:** interpretation, not source truth.  
**Do not use as a final Kit personality specification.**

This document captures what the current work suggests and, equally importantly, what the S3 work has taught us **not** to assume.

## 1. The project is larger than "how Brendon DMs"

The archive contains evidence about at least:

- moment-to-moment DM judgment;
- campaign architecture;
- large-scale multiplayer operations;
- encounter design;
- homebrew mechanical design;
- race/class/subclass design;
- worldbuilding;
- improvisation;
- revision behavior;
- failure diagnosis;
- creative experimentation with the form of D&D itself.

A future Kit may need different parts of this archive for different jobs.

Running a solo dungeon and helping design a future Roanoke season are related tasks, but they do not require identical priors.

## 2. S3 is a case study, not the answer key

The S3 research produced strong local evidence, but it is easy to harden those events into "Brendon doctrine."

That is not justified yet.

Roanoke's operating conditions matter:
- dozens of concurrent players;
- sometimes roughly 30–100 people;
- multiple DMs/moderators;
- asynchronous play;
- many simultaneous channels/scenes;
- scheduled campaign infrastructure.

Some behaviors may be scale adaptations rather than universal DM preferences.

## 3. Chronology matters because Brendon changed

Early Roanoke, S3, later campaigns, and Earthfall should not be flattened into an average.

Future synthesis should distinguish:

- **persistent traits** — survive across years and formats;
- **evolved practices** — recognizable concern, improved technique;
- **format-specific adaptations** — work because of campaign constraints;
- **abandoned/superseded practices** — things later work appears to reject.

A statement such as "he used to do this and later stopped" may be more useful to Kit than a timeless rule.

## 4. Emergence matters

The interpersonal layer later described as the **dating sim** was not simply an authored campaign feature. It emerged from player behavior.

That distinction is important.

A candidate developmental pattern to test is:

> emergent player behavior → Brendon notices sustained value → makes room for it / gives it consequences → later recognizes or formalizes the pattern

Do not yet universalize this from S3. Search for comparable examples across seasons.

## 5. Current S3 judgment hypotheses

The strongest current hypotheses include:

- prepared content is evaluated at delivery rather than protected simply because it exists;
- unresolved existing threads are often preferred over arbitrary replacement when momentum needs help;
- meaningful player investment can justify additional preparation;
- some boundaries are prerequisites to adjudication rather than things dice can override;
- danger that lands as unfair can trigger a telegraphing correction rather than consequence erasure;
- preparation can deliberately reserve adaptive capacity for information that will only exist later;
- Brendon often diagnoses missing **play function** rather than merely missing word count;
- live adaptation may happen outside the canonical master document.

These remain S3-derived until tested elsewhere.

## 6. Research must include restraint

Current cases over-sample visible interventions because changes are easier to notice than non-actions.

A balanced model needs examples of:
- self-sustaining play left alone;
- interesting material not promoted;
- prep deliberately left unchanged;
- cool ideas rejected;
- hooks allowed to fail;
- moments where restraint was the successful DM choice.

Without this, Kit would learn to constantly "improve" functioning play.

## 7. The archive may reveal a broader creative methodology

The 2018 Bowling Event is an early lead.

It does not merely set a D&D story in a bowling alley. It maps the physical act of bowling into the resolution engine:
- knocked-down pins become damage;
- standing pins become incoming damage;
- bowling outcomes trigger monster abilities;
- class abilities become physical/social bowling behaviors;
- frames structure encounters;
- gold and competition create a secondary scoring loop.

This suggests a broader research question:

> What kinds of boundaries does Brendon repeatedly try to push, and how does he turn a strange premise into something playable?

That question should be applied across the full corpus, not answered from bowling alone.

## 8. Kit should not be specified too early

Potential future uses of the corpus include:
- human-readable synthesis for discussion;
- retrieval;
- evaluation;
- judgment/preference seeding;
- creative apprenticeship;
- design precedent;
- campaign-format reconstruction.

The correct implementation should be chosen after broader research, not before.

The desired outcome is an AI that is **interesting and useful to Brendon** in more than one role:
- running a solo game;
- helping design future campaigns/seasons;
- challenging design assumptions;
- recalling relevant historical experiments;
- noticing recurring failures;
- making genuinely new work from the lineage rather than cloning old campaigns.
