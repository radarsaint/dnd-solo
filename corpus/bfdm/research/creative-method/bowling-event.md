## What the source actually does

The design converts **real-world bowling performance into the D&D resolution system**.

Observed mechanics include:

- Every pin knocked down deals damage to the encounter enemy.
- Every pin left standing deals damage to the player.
- A strike is treated as critical damage (20 damage).
- A spare grants gold.
- Encounters occupy a set number of bowling frames.
- If a monster dies before its allotted frames end, later pin results can become loot.
- Character abilities can be used once per encounter.
- The Rug of Smothering keys its grapple to an odd number of pins left after the first ball.
- The Treant's damage to a building scales from pins left standing.
- Other encounters use damage types, resistances, weaknesses, visibility thresholds, and phased boss effects.
- Class abilities are translated into physical/social behavior at the lane: declarations, gestures, timing relative to another bowler, predictions, and other embodied actions.
- The event was intended for five groups of five adventurers, with total gold creating an inter-group competition over ten frames.

## Why this matters

The unusual part is not simply "D&D themed bowling."

The design treats bowling itself as the input device and resolution engine, then translates D&D concepts into affordances of that physical activity.

That makes it useful evidence for a broader question about Brendon's creative process:

> When he pushes the boundary of D&D, how does he preserve enough recognizable game structure that the strange format remains playable?

## Candidate observations to test elsewhere

These are hypotheses only:

1. **Use the external activity directly rather than simulate it.**  
   Instead of rolling Dexterity to represent bowling, actual bowling determines game state.

2. **Translate core concepts rather than preserve literal rules.**  
   Damage, criticals, healing, class identity, resistances, conditions, boss phases, loot, and competition survive, but their implementation changes.

3. **Exploit the medium's native states.**  
   Odd/even standing pins, strikes, spares, frames, gutter balls, and lane timing become mechanical hooks.

4. **Make performance/social behavior part of play.**  
   Some class features require declarations, gestures, or performative behavior before/during bowling.

## What this does NOT yet prove

It does not establish that Brendon always prefers:
- physical mechanics;
- minigames;
- comedy;
- D&D-rule replacement;
- competitive scoring.

Those claims require comparison with other experiments across the archive.
