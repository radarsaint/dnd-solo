# Roanoke S3 → Empire City S4 Judgment Comparison v1

**Status:** provisional developmental comparison.  
**Scope:** two consecutive Roanoke-lineage campaigns with broadly similar persistent, large, multi-DM Discord operating conditions.

Purpose: distinguish recurring judgment from one-season artifacts without promoting Roanoke-specific operations into universal Brendon behavior.

## Evidence base

Season 3:
- `research/roanoke-s3/decision-cases-v1.md`
- `research/roanoke-s3/longitudinal-decision-cases-v2.md`

Season 4 / Empire City:
- `research/empire-city/decision-cases-v1.md`
- `research/empire-city/longitudinal-decision-cases-v1.md`
- `research/empire-city/player-feedback-v1.md`

This comparison uses already source-traceable cases. It does not treat one derived case file as independent confirmation of another when both rest on the same source lineage.

# 1. Strong persistence candidate: preserve the intended experience, not the prepared form

## S3

Several S3 cases show prepared encounters being treated as conditional:

- `BDC-S3-003` — a preplanned fight is rejected when four depleted players no longer support it.
- `BDC-S3-020` — a dangerous tree encounter becomes exploration-only if turnout cannot support combat.
- `BDC-S3-L08` — a boss-sized encounter is replaced by a smaller responsive encounter that preserves the kind of play the available players still want.

## S4

`BDC-S4-005` shows the same decision structure with different content.

The planned airships-versus-Kong set piece is canceled because:
- the group has only one armed ship;
- Brendon is not sufficiently prepared;
- the event can be preserved for later when its prerequisites exist.

S4-L01 scales the same logic up to campaign architecture: once late-game attendance no longer supports the form of content prepared for it, the finale itself is reworked and moved.

## Candidate judgment

> Preparation creates options and intended experiences. It does not create an obligation to deliver the prepared form after its assumptions stop being true.

**Persistence confidence:** high within the Roanoke lineage.  
**Generalization scope:** format-specific/cross-campaign candidate, not yet general-current-practice.

# 2. Strong persistence candidate: player investment can increase the importance of material after play begins

## S3

- `BDC-S3-008` — emergent player material is promoted into bespoke future content.
- `BDC-S3-L01` — repeated interest in Victor transforms incidental set dressing into a multi-day invitational story.

The signal is not simply that a player asks once. Repeated attention or an action that creates meaningful new story value changes what is worth preparing.

## S4

- `BDC-S4-004` / `BDC-S4-L02` — a player responds to destroyed Ferrytown by trying to rebuild a damaged location.
- Brendon explicitly says he had not planned this, recognizes rebuilding as “a healing thing,” authorizes access, and starts driving the resulting narrative.

The form differs from S3:
- S3 promotes curiosity about an incidental NPC.
- S4 promotes a player-created response to campaign consequences.

The underlying decision structure is similar: **live investment changes preparation priority.**

## Candidate judgment

> Treat player attention and player-authored responses as evidence about where latent story value exists. Promote the material when the response creates more meaning than the original scope assigned to it.

**Persistence confidence:** high within the Roanoke lineage.  
**Generalization scope:** cross-campaign candidate.

# 3. Very strong developmental candidate: rewrite architecture when its operating assumptions fail

This is one of the clearest recurrences in the current corpus.

## S3

`BDC-S3-017` / `BDC-S3-L07`:

Prepared assumption:
- four active factions;
- four separate story lanes and endgame functions.

Failure:
- two DMs become less active;
- the remaining game collapses into a bipartisan winner-take-all structure;
- the structure starts producing social conflict contrary to the intended campaign experience.

Response:
- rewrite the ending architecture;
- preserve distinct faction contribution without exclusivity;
- audit and remove dependent mechanics that no longer make sense.

## S4

`BDC-S4-L01`:

Prepared assumption:
- late game retains enough population to support large events;
- Brendon later says remaining content was scaled expecting roughly 30 people still present.

Failure:
- staff report roughly 15 active players including mods;
- actual quests are drawing 5–8;
- large prepared events are no longer viable.

Response:
- discuss truncating the season with the DM group;
- rework the final fight;
- move the finale earlier;
- preserve RP afterward;
- immediately state two S5 architecture changes: advertise and shorten the season.

## Difference across seasons

S3 repairs a failed **staff/faction topology** inside the current campaign.

S4 repairs a failed **population/duration topology** and carries the lesson into the next campaign's stated design.

That makes S4 particularly valuable developmental evidence: the correction is not only reactive. It becomes a future-design rule while the current season is still ending.

## Candidate judgment

> When the assumptions under a campaign structure fail, change the structure at the level where the failure occurred. Do not demand that players or collaborators continue performing a design built for a population, staffing pattern, or social condition that no longer exists.

**Persistence confidence:** very high within Roanoke.  
**Developmental significance:** high.  
**Generalization scope:** campaign-architecture candidate; needs non-Roanoke testing.

# 4. Persistent Roanoke operating principle: scale is part of game design, not merely logistics

S3 already shows:
- encounter mode changing with turnout (`BDC-S3-020`);
- faction architecture changing with DM availability (`BDC-S3-017`);
- event scheduling yielding to actual table state (`BDC-S3-016`).

S4 makes the scale dependence unusually explicit:
- “Having 5-8 players a quest has really killed my ability to run the events I have planned.”
- late content had been scaled assuming roughly 30 people remaining.

The important inference is bounded:

> In these large persistent campaigns, population and staffing are mechanical/design inputs because they determine which encounter forms, faction structures, and event scales can function.

This should **not** be turned into a general rule for ordinary 4–6-player tables without other evidence.

**Confidence:** very high for Roanoke-format operation.

# 5. S4 makes collaborative authority unusually visible

S4 contains two strong cases not yet matched with an equally clean S3 case.

## Protect another DM's active plan

`BDC-S4-006`:
Brendon refuses to approve something that may interfere with another DM's plan until that DM weighs in.

## Delivered shared canon can revise private intent

`BDC-S4-007` / `BDC-S4-L03`:
Brendon's private leyline sequence collides with another DM's delivered event. He accepts a coherent combined account and plans a public clarification rather than using central authority to restore his private sequence.

## Candidate judgment

> In a multi-DM campaign, campaign ownership does not mean every decision is centrally owned. Protect collaborator decision space, and treat delivered shared play as evidence that can revise private prep.

**Evidence confidence:** high in S4.  
**Persistence confidence:** unresolved; S3 comparison still needed.

## Important counterevidence

End-of-season player feedback explicitly asks for **more communication between DMs**.

That prevents a flattering overgeneralization.

The correct current reading is:
- S4 contains clear examples of Brendon protecting collaborator autonomy and reconciling shared canon;
- those behaviors were not sufficient to make coordination consistently successful from the player perspective.

This tension is useful research data.

# 6. S4 continuity boundary candidate: shared narrative resources are protected from private appropriation

Two S4 cases expose a related boundary:

- `BDC-S4-002` — a player may be a revolutionary assassin, but claiming a parent-like relationship with a central George Washington goes too far.
- `BDC-S4-003` — players may reference prior Roanoke material, but direct privileged links to famous prior figures are restricted and references are intended to be distributed in “equal measure.”

The repeated decision is not “say no to backstory.”

In both cases Brendon:
1. identifies the part that overclaims shared narrative authority;
2. preserves the rest of the player's concept;
3. offers a narrower route that still participates in the setting.

## Candidate judgment

> A player may enter shared history without pre-owning its central figures or scarce continuity rewards.

**Evidence confidence:** high in S4.  
**Persistence confidence:** unresolved; no clean S3 comparison has yet been established.

# 7. S4 outcome evidence supports responsiveness but exposes operational costs

The small explicit feedback sample is not representative polling, but it provides useful outcome checks.

Players praise:
- shaping the world;
- DMs running with character choices and chosen quests;
- memorable chaos and NPC interactions.

Players criticize:
- the long mid-season break and loss of activity;
- encounter balance;
- DM communication;
- some large-combat procedure.

This matters methodologically.

The corpus should not infer:
> “responsive adaptation was present, therefore the campaign operation was successful.”

The stronger model is:
> individual judgment can be good while system-level execution still has failure modes.

That distinction is important for Kit. A capable DM runtime cannot substitute isolated “good decisions” for monitoring whether the whole game is functioning.

# Current developmental picture

After S3 + S4, the best-supported recurring decision structures are:

1. **Prepared content is conditional on live state.**
2. **Player investment can reprioritize what gets prepared.**
3. **Failed operating assumptions justify structural rewrites.**
4. **Scale/staffing are first-class design inputs in persistent large-format play.**
5. **Corrections should preserve the purpose of a design where possible rather than preserving its original implementation.**

S4 additionally provides strong candidates around:
- collaborator sovereignty within a shared campaign;
- delivered shared canon outranking private headcanon;
- protecting shared narrative capital from privileged player backstory claims;
- explicitly turning current-season failure into next-season design.

# What remains unproven

This pass still cannot establish that these are general Brendon DM rules.

S3 and S4 share too much format:
- Roanoke lineage;
- persistent Discord;
- large population;
- multiple DMs/moderators;
- simultaneous scenes and events.

The next decisive comparison should come from a materially different operating environment—Bastion/Redoubt, At War's End where applicable, Earthfall, or another smaller/synchronous campaign.

If the same decision structures survive there, the case for general DM judgment becomes substantially stronger.
