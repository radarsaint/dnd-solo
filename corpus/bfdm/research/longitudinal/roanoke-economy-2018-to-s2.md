# Early Longitudinal Case — Roanoke Economy: 2018 → Season 2

**Research class:** developmental design case  
**Confidence:** high for the local change; medium for broader generalization  
**Sources:** *The Rowing Oak* (2018) + *Roanoke Season 2* revision 793 (2019)

## 2018 design

The early Roanoke material builds player interdependence through:
- many specialist jobs;
- real-time/offline production;
- resource scarcity;
- food requirements;
- barter;
- unfilled roles creating actual shortages.

The player-facing rules explicitly say there are more roles than people and that what the colony cannot produce, it must survive without.

## 2019 retrospective diagnosis

In `Roanoke Season 2`, revision 793 dated 2019-03-07 and attributed by Drive revision metadata to Brendon Faulkner, Brendon evaluates the previous system.

He identifies two separate failures:

1. **The job system became a chore.**  
   Counting hours not played in order to calculate work output did not make crafting fun.

2. **Removing gold did not create the intended cooperation.**  
   Brendon states that removing gold was his choice to encourage “working together,” but players cooperated anyway while the loss of currency removed the useful language of trade. Some desirable jobs were heavily selected while necessary jobs went empty.

He proposes changing the system rather than defending the original premise:
- one crafting attempt per day;
- vocation bonuses;
- quality rolls;
- bankable work;
- return of gold;
- more NPCs to cover structurally necessary roles.

## What this case actually supports

This is unusually valuable because the later document states:
- the earlier design goal;
- what players actually did;
- what failed experientially;
- the replacement direction.

A narrow supported interpretation is:

> When a system designed to force social cooperation creates bookkeeping friction without producing unique social value, Brendon is willing to abandon the mechanism while preserving the underlying goal through a simpler structure.

It also shows that he distinguishes **desired player behavior** from the mechanic originally chosen to cause it. Players already found ways to cooperate; therefore the mechanic that was supposed to force cooperation was no longer justified by that purpose.

## What it does not yet prove

Do not generalize this into:
- “Brendon always simplifies systems”;
- “Brendon dislikes simulation”;
- “gold is always preferable to barter”;
- “player complaints automatically trigger redesign.”

The evidence is specific to a persistent-game economy whose bookkeeping became work.

## Research value for Kit

This is the kind of developmental example the full corpus should seek:

> intended experience → implemented system → observed player behavior/friction → explicit diagnosis → revised system

It is more informative than treating either the 2018 rules or the 2019 rules as timeless preference.
