# Research Questions

These questions are intentionally broader than "how does Brendon DM?" They are candidate lenses for the full corpus once source coverage is sufficient.

## DM judgment
- What reliably catches Brendon's attention?
- What makes him intervene?
- What makes him leave functioning play alone?
- What makes a player-created detail become story-bearing?
- When does he preserve a prepared encounter, alter it, or throw it away?
- What makes danger feel fair or unfair to him?
- What kinds of failure does he preserve?
- What does he reward, mechanically or narratively?
- What makes him decide that a session, mechanic, NPC, or campaign structure is not working?

## Creative problem solving
- What kinds of boundaries of D&D does he repeatedly try to push?
- How does a strange premise become something actually playable?
- Does he begin with fiction, mechanics, player behavior, physical constraints, or desired experience?
- What does he simplify?
- What complexity does he deliberately preserve?
- How does he translate an external activity or genre into D&D mechanics?

## Mechanical design
- How does he design custom races, classes, subclasses, items, skills, and subsystems?
- Which mechanics survive revision?
- Which are repeatedly removed or simplified?
- What tradeoffs does he make between simulation, flavor, usability, balance, and spectacle?
- When does he invent a reusable procedure instead of adjudicating case-by-case?

## Campaign architecture
- How does he design for 4–6 players versus 30–100 concurrent players?
- How does he allocate authored structure versus adaptive capacity?
- How are factions, schedules, locations, NPCs, and invitationals used at different scales?
- How does he design persistent worlds so player actions can matter without requiring constant direct DM control?

## Emergence
- Which major features began as player behavior rather than authored design?
- Which emergent behaviors were later named or formalized?
- Which did he intentionally design for in later campaigns?
- Which were one-off accidents?

## Development over time
- What would early Brendon do that later Brendon stopped doing?
- What practices persist from early Roanoke through Earthfall?
- Where does later work explicitly correct an earlier design assumption?
- What changes appear to come from experience rather than format?
- Where does increased skill show up in prep, delivery, encounter design, pacing, or restraint?

## Failure and revision
- What failures does Brendon notice immediately?
- Which failures require player feedback before he notices them?
- What kind of correction follows each type?
- Does he patch locally, redesign a subsystem, or change architecture?
- How often do later designs preserve the lesson from an earlier failure?

## Taste
- What repeatedly makes him laugh?
- What kinds of weirdness does he consider worth developing?
- What kinds of material does he abandon despite effort already spent?
- What kinds of NPCs, rewards, challenges, reveals, and player behavior does he return to?
- Which preferences remain stable even as technique improves?

## Negative space
- What does he choose not to respond to?
- What player activity is allowed to remain player-owned?
- What cool ideas does he reject?
- What prep survives unchanged despite player pressure?
- When does he decide that no intervention is the correct intervention?

## Transfer to Kit
Do not answer these until the research base is broad enough:

- Which conclusions should become Kit's initial priors?
- Which should remain searchable precedents rather than personality?
- Which are campaign-format skills Kit should invoke only when relevant?
- Which old practices should be taught as historical mistakes rather than templates?
- How should Kit's own later table history override or modify inherited Brendon priors?
- What should remain available only as design reference for tasks such as building a future Roanoke-scale season?
