## BDC-S4-001 — Deny an exception when it removes intended vulnerability and cannot scale fairly

**Situation.** A player asked to live in a private apartment rather than use the campaign's normal vulnerable home/airship structure.

**Live evidence.** `863492563784040539` — 2021-07-10T18:51:10.122000Z — DM Chat / `dm-transparency-log`.

**What Brendon noticed.** The request was not merely cosmetic. A safer fixed home would remove a pressure the housing design was meant to create, and approving one exception would create a precedent for many similar requests.

**What mattered.** intended vulnerability; meaningful risk; consistency at population scale.

**Intervention.** He denied the request and explained both reasons: homes were intended to be vulnerable, and a one-off exception could not be offered fairly at scale.

**Representative line.** “Your home is meant to be vulnerable and there is meant to be risk associated with. And if I give out one, a million others will ask for the same thing.”

**Reusable judgment candidate.** If a requested exception erases a deliberately designed pressure and cannot be offered consistently to comparable players, preserve the pressure rather than treating the request as harmless flavor.

**Claim scope.** campaign-specific; cross-campaign candidate.

**Confidence.** high.

## BDC-S4-002 — Preserve the player's premise while trimming a claim on a central shared NPC

**Situation.** A player proposed being a revolutionary assassin connected to George Washington. Brendon initially accepted the assassin premise while warning that Washington would be used sparsely. The backstory later escalated the relationship into Washington treating the character like a son.

**Live evidence.**
- `863889244996632587` — 2021-07-11T21:07:26.293000Z — DM Chat / `dm-transparency-log`.
- `863890123526242325` — 2021-07-11T21:10:55.751000Z — same channel.

**What Brendon noticed.** The first version created a usable connection to the campaign. The later version claimed privileged narrative ownership over a central shared figure.

**What mattered.** player concept; shared-world authority; keeping central campaign figures available to the whole game.

**Intervention.** He kept the revolutionary-assassin concept and narrowed only the overreaching relationship.

**Representative line.** “you can totally be an assassin for the revolution, but like a son to Washington might be a bit far. Normally I wouldn't step this far in to work on editing backstory. But general Washington is a fairly central figure.”

**Reusable judgment candidate.** When a player backstory crosses from participating in the shared setting to pre-owning a central campaign figure, preserve the viable premise and cut the specific claim that constrains shared play.

**Claim scope.** campaign-specific.

**Confidence.** high.

## BDC-S4-003 — Treat legacy continuity as shared campaign capital, then look for a fair compromise

**Situation.** Players wanted personal backstory links to figures and material from prior Roanoke play.

**Live evidence.** `864911042601811980` — 2021-07-14T16:47:41.826000Z — DM Chat / `dm-transparency-log`.

**What Brendon noticed.** Nostalgia for prior campaigns could become a private advantage or privileged continuity claim if individual players attached themselves directly to famous prior characters.

**What mattered.** equal access to legacy references; DM control over shared continuity; preserving nostalgia without letting one player appropriate it.

**Intervention.** He rejected direct privileged links he had also barred for other players, while explicitly offering broader setting references and a compromise that would not grant a special exception.

**Representative line.** “references belong to the dms to give out in equal measure nods to the things and moments.”

**Reusable judgment candidate.** Treat shared campaign history as common narrative capital. If a player's backstory would privatize that capital, redirect toward a version that preserves the reference without creating unequal ownership.

**Claim scope.** Roanoke-lineage specific; potentially relevant to continuity management more generally.

**Confidence.** high.

## BDC-S4-004 — Accept an unplanned player response when it creates meaningful recovery

**Situation.** After destructive events in Ferrytown, a player asked to clear rubble and help rebuild a damaged location. This was not part of Brendon's plan.

**Live evidence.**
- `865565663296225280` — 2021-07-16T12:08:55.562000Z — collaborator relays the request in Admin Chat / `a-fountain-of-player-tears`.
- `865565818481279018` — 2021-07-16T12:09:32.561000Z — Brendon.
- `865565932783796255` — 2021-07-16T12:09:59.813000Z — Brendon grants permission.
- `865566464831258674` — 2021-07-16T12:12:06.663000Z — Brendon gives full support.
- `865567436970786836` — 2021-07-16T12:15:58.439000Z — Brendon says he will drive the resulting narrative.

**What Brendon noticed.** The player was responding to destruction as a persistent change to a place they cared about, not merely waiting for the next authored event.

**What mattered.** player investment; recovery after loss; the feeling that the world can be acted upon and repaired.

**Intervention.** He approved special access to the damaged location and converted the spontaneous rebuilding impulse into supported ongoing narrative.

**Representative line.** “I mean, I hadn't planned on it. But players helping rebuild the server is a healing thing.”

**Observed result.** The player's repair action was allowed into a location that was otherwise unavailable, and staff were told to support rebuilding activity.

**Reusable judgment candidate.** When players invent a response to consequences that creates ownership, recovery, or richer continuity, treat the unplanned response as candidate story material rather than protecting the original plan from it.

**Claim scope.** campaign-specific; strong cross-S3 comparison candidate.

**Confidence.** high.

## BDC-S4-005 — Defer prepared spectacle when both fictional prerequisites and DM readiness fail

**Situation.** Brendon had planned an airships-versus-Kong fight. At delivery time, the player group had only one armed ship and Brendon was not going to be sufficiently prepared.

**Live evidence.** `866777909787623465` — 2021-07-19T20:25:57.648000Z — DM Chat / `admin-to-dm-notes`.

**What Brendon noticed.** The event's assumptions had failed on two sides simultaneously: the players had not acquired the expected resources, and the DM was not ready to run the spectacle at the desired level.

**What mattered.** encounter readiness; fictional support; quality of delivery; preserving the event for a state where it could work.

**Intervention.** He canceled the fight for that moment, considered bringing it back later as an invitational once more airships existed, and substituted smaller side games rather than forcing the set piece.

**Representative line.** “The group only has one armed ship, and I'm not going to be prepped in time. I might run it as an invitational later when they have more airships.”

**Reusable judgment candidate.** Prepared spectacle is conditional. If the live world state and DM readiness no longer support the encounter's intended experience, defer or rescope it instead of delivering a diminished version merely because it was prepared.

**Cross-S3 lead.** Compare with `BDC-S3-003`, where a preplanned fight was also subordinated to actual party state.

**Claim scope.** cross-campaign candidate.

**Confidence.** high.

## BDC-S4-006 — Protect collaborator plans before exercising central authority

**Situation.** Staff asked Brendon to approve something that might overlap another DM's planned material.

**Live evidence.** `865364564056735776` — 2021-07-15T22:49:49.767000Z — Mod Chat / `dm-to-mod-notes`.

**What Brendon noticed.** He had enough authority to approve the request, but doing so could silently invalidate or interfere with another DM's work.

**What mattered.** collaborator agency; continuity across simultaneous content; avoiding unilateral ownership of a multi-DM campaign.

**Intervention.** He declined to sign off until the other DM could weigh in.

**Representative line.** “I don't want to sign off on anything that could interfere with anything [the other DM] has planned, so lets get her input.”

**Reusable judgment candidate.** In a shared-DM environment, authority does not erase ownership boundaries. When a decision may collide with another DM's active plan, resolve the dependency before approving it.

**Claim scope.** multi-DM format-specific candidate.

**Confidence.** high.

## BDC-S4-007 — Treat private headcanon as provisional when the shared live record has moved

**Situation.** Brendon had an internal plan for when the leylines would be restored. Another DM had run related live material, creating uncertainty about the current state.

**Live evidence.**
- `869309519640010852` — 2021-07-26T20:05:40.472000Z — DM Chat / `fountain-of-tacos`.
- `869310491187617892` — 2021-07-26T20:09:32.107000Z.
- `869310601002909736` — 2021-07-26T20:09:58.289000Z.

**What Brendon noticed.** His internal sequence and the collaboratively delivered sequence were not perfectly aligned, but they could be reconciled without invalidating what had already happened in play.

**What mattered.** shared canon; collaborator contributions; player-facing clarity.

**Intervention.** He accepted a compatible interpretation of the events and chose to clarify the resulting state publicly instead of insisting that the private plan outranked the live record.

**Representative line.** “My internal head canon was that the machinery siphoning the ley power was destroyed Friday and the mender done did the mending yesterday…”

**Observed result.** The staff converged on a coherent explanation and Brendon said he would post an announcement clearing it up.

**Reusable judgment candidate.** In collaborative play, private intent is provisional. When the live shared record can be reconciled coherently, preserve delivered play and update the public state rather than retconning toward private prep.

**Claim scope.** multi-DM format-specific; strong corpus-governance analogue but do not conflate the two.

**Confidence.** high.

## BDC-S4-008 — Ask what the campaign now needs before filling the final weeks with more content

**Situation.** Entering the final two weeks, Brendon opened a DM discussion about current direction rather than assuming the original trajectory should continue unchanged.

**Live evidence.**
- `871838229811298475` — 2021-08-02T19:33:51.958000Z — DM Chat / `fountain-of-tacos`.
- `871843341761904651` — 2021-08-02T19:54:10.742000Z.
- `871843455805030441` — 2021-08-02T19:54:37.932000Z.
- `871946925778284574` — 2021-08-03T02:45:47.096000Z.

**What Brendon noticed.** The late campaign had personal stories worth foregrounding, while the city lacked a named villain who could interact directly with those stories.

**What mattered.** personal payoff; legible opposition; final-act structure; collaborator perspective.

**Intervention.** He asked the other DMs where their material was going, proposed leaning harder into personal Kingsbridge stories, identified the named-villain gap, and then outlined a sequence of internal corruption before the external final conflict.

**Representative line.** “What we don't have right now is a named villain to interact with the city more.”

**Reusable judgment candidate.** Late-campaign preparation should diagnose what the accumulated story is missing, not merely add more material. Look for unresolved personal threads, weak opposition, or absent connective structure and prepare toward those gaps.

**Claim scope.** campaign-specific developmental lead.

**Confidence.** high.

## Early cross-campaign signals — not yet conclusions

Two S4 cases deserve direct comparison with S3:

- `BDC-S4-004` ↔ `BDC-S3-008`: unplanned player investment is promoted into supported future content.
- `BDC-S4-005` ↔ `BDC-S3-003`: a prepared encounter is subordinate to the actual state at delivery time.

That recurrence is evidence worth testing. It is not yet sufficient to label either pattern a timeless Brendon rule.
