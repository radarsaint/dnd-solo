# Empire City — End-of-Season Player Feedback v1

Purpose: preserve the small explicit feedback sample as **player evaluation evidence**, not Brendon-authored evidence.

## Source

Private Empire City Discord:
- category: `Things you should read.`
- channel: `feedback`
- feedback request begins at message `880341383569883167` on 2021-08-26.

Only seven messages survive in this channel. This is a small self-selected sample and must not be treated as representative polling of the full player population.

Player identities are not necessary for the BFDM research question and are omitted here.

## Positive signals

Player feedback explicitly praised:

- the campaign's social group and amount of memorable story;
- Arcaniabot;
- the long-rest mechanism as a useful brake on nonstop play;
- learning new proficiencies;
- crafting as a compelling idea even when difficult to understand;
- participating in government/world-shaping;
- DMs following character choices, NPC interests, and quests players chose to engage;
- Brendon's small chaos/comedy moments and memorable antagonistic NPCs.

Research relevance:

The praise for world-shaping and DMs following player choices is an independent player-side outcome signal for the kind of responsiveness seen in cases such as `BDC-S4-004`. It does not prove why Brendon made those decisions.

## Negative signals

The feedback also identifies several operational failures:

### Mid-season break / inactivity

A player reports:
- the break lasted longer than expected;
- active population fell during/after it;
- players felt unable to organize their own activities during the downtime;
- low-intensity progression such as crafting or skill learning could have preserved momentum.

This independently aligns with the internal DM discussion on August 19 that attendance had clearly fallen. It does not by itself establish the full cause of that decline.

### Encounter balance

A player reports:
- some encounters felt substantially over-tuned;
- deaths were frequent enough to be associated with the balance problem;
- a dedicated balancing role might have helped;
- round-count conditions could make large combats drag, with player-turn-count triggers suggested as more predictable.

This is useful evaluation evidence for later mechanical/operations research. It should not be converted directly into a claim about Brendon's judgment without examining the actual encounters and DM decisions involved.

### DM coordination

A player explicitly wanted more communication between DMs.

This is an important counterweight to the strong examples in the decision corpus where Brendon actively protects collaborator plans or reconciles shared canon. The presence of good coordination decisions did not mean coordination was consistently successful across the campaign.

### Death design

One player says S4 death felt more like a transformation mechanism than a source of risk, and explicitly interprets this as a response to Season 3.

That statement is evidence of the player's perception only. The claimed S3 → S4 design causality still requires Brendon/prep evidence before it can be promoted as a developmental fact.

## Additional small signal

A later player comment says that naming enemy creatures during combat had been useful and should have been done more often.

This may be relevant to future interface/large-combat research, but the sample is too small to generalize.

## Research use

This channel is best used as an outcome/evaluation layer:

> DM decision/preparation → delivered play → player-reported experience

It should not be treated as a substitute for live behavioral evidence or as a representative survey.

## Triangulation against preparation and live DM records

### Extended intermission and momentum

The S4 Master Timeline schedules:

- intermission / Continental Congress: July 31–August 6;
- Week 4 beginning August 7.

Actual live delivery remained substantially paused longer:

- `870550505108176927` — July 30 — Brendon refers to “2 weeks of break.”
- `873356284462768198` — August 7 — Brendon freezes Kingsbridge and most of The City and explicitly says the pause is meant to be a break from the game.
- `875334885831766036` — August 12 — announcement says Week 4 will officially begin Saturday, August 14.
- `876009753019559936` — August 14 — Brendon reopens normal play.

The player complaint that the break lasted longer than expected therefore has strong support in the delivered calendar relative to the original master timeline.

The same player says active population fell during/after the break. Brendon later independently identifies a clear attendance drop (`877727568147677214`).

**Causal boundary:** the sources support overlap between the extended break and attendance decline, plus a player perception that the break hurt momentum. They do **not** prove the break was the sole or primary cause. Brendon separately identifies non-advertising as a mistake (`877729415042322464`).

### Encounter intent versus delivered balance

Brendon's live record contains a coherent intended challenge model:

- `865722271108431873` — ordinary Redcoat encounters are not intended to be unwinnable; the goal is for characters to hate Redcoats, not for players to hate DMs.
- `863846533170593803` / `863925435624194068` — unusually lethal/high-risk content is explicitly advertised as such.
- `866253241780011011` — Brendon asks for party max HP to check encounter balance.
- `868233291919134720` — asks for airship sheets so he can double-check balance.
- `877244547342680074` — promises to rebalance Dark Violet according to actual player count.
- `879907648231837736` — asks for final-fight player count to double-check balance.

The negative feedback nevertheless reports:
- some encounters felt substantially overtuned;
- frequent deaths were associated with the problem;
- round-count conditions made some large combats drag;
- a dedicated balancing role might have helped.

This is important outcome evidence.

**Interpretation:** S4 shows that an explicit fairness philosophy and active balancing checks did not reliably produce the intended experience at campaign scale. Later BFDM/Kit work should learn from both the intended model and the delivered failure signal.

### Distributed authorship versus synchronization

The live record contains good collaboration practices:
- Brendon protects collaborator-owned plans rather than approving conflicting content unilaterally.
- `BDC-S4-L03` records a leyline-state conflict that is reconciled after delivery instead of retconned.
- during the attendance crisis Brendon explicitly tells co-DMs that the campaign is as much theirs as his.

The player feedback still asks for more communication between DMs.

The leyline case itself also demonstrates the synchronization problem:
- Brendon expected restoration after his airship event;
- another DM had already delivered a leyline-repair event;
- the team then had to reconcile the shared state.

**Interpretation:** distributed authorship was a creative strength with real synchronization overhead. Successful individual reconciliation decisions should not be mistaken for evidence that the coordination system was consistently sufficient.

### Research consequence

For S4 evaluation, distinguish:

> intended design -> operational practice -> delivered player experience

All three layers matter.

A stated design principle should not be promoted as successful simply because it is thoughtful. Player feedback is one of the checks on whether the principle survived scale, coordination, pacing, and actual encounter delivery.
