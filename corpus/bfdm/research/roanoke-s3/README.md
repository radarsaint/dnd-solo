# Roanoke Season 3 Research

Roanoke Season 3 is currently the richest live-play dataset in the BFDM corpus, but it must not be mistaken for a complete model of Brendon.

## Why S3 is valuable

The archive combines:
- a large Discord harvest;
- pre-season planning;
- week-specific run documents;
- change logs;
- Google Drive revision history;
- live player/DM interaction;
- post-prep operational edits.

That makes it unusually good for reconstructing decision chains and testing causal methods.

## Why S3 is dangerous to overgeneralize

Roanoke sometimes involved roughly **30–100 concurrent players**, multiple DMs/moderators, asynchronous channels, scheduled events, factions, and simultaneous activity.

A practice can therefore be:
- excellent Roanoke operations;
- evidence of Brendon's general judgment;
- both;
- or impossible to classify without comparison.

The volume of S3 evidence also creates a weighting problem: evidence density must not masquerade as importance.

## The three passes

### v1 — Decision cases
A set of local decision reconstructions:
situation → what Brendon noticed → competing values → intervention → observed result → reusable judgment.

### v2 — Longitudinal cases
Follows chains across time:
prepared expectation → live signal → intervention/adaptation → downstream consequence.

### v3 — Revision family
Uses Drive revision history to separate:
- prep-driven change;
- live operational edits;
- live response;
- prepared adaptability;
- unresolved/mixed causality.

The v3 pass also corrected at least one earlier interpretation: lower authored density in Week 5 was substantially planned adaptive capacity rather than simply a live emergency decompression.

## Current use

Treat S3 as:
- a methodology testbed;
- one historical case study;
- a source of hypotheses to test elsewhere.

Do **not** yet use it as a universal Brendon doctrine.
