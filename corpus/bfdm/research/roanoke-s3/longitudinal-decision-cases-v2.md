# Roanoke Season 3 — Longitudinal Brendon DM Decision Cases v2

Purpose: recover **how a prepared campaign changes after players touch it**. These cases follow a chain over hours or days: prepared expectation → live signal → Brendon intervention → downstream change. They are intended to teach DM judgment, not Brendon’s prose style and not Roanoke lore.

## Source and method

- Planning baseline: `BCS-000045` — *Roanoke Season 3 Rough Draft*.
- Live record: private `radarsaint/bfdm-corpus`, `discord/roanoke-season-3/roanoke-season-3.sqlite` (197,013 messages).
- Brendon Discord identity: `DM radar` / `bfdm`, user `313689699627696139`.
- Message IDs and timestamps are retained so every inference can be reconstructed against the raw corpus.
- Player messages are paraphrased by default. Brendon’s own text is retained where it directly exposes the decision.
- Sensitive player disclosures are deliberately excluded when they are not necessary to recover Brendon’s judgment.

## What this pass is testing

The narrower question is: **What makes Brendon decide that live play has become important enough to change what he prepared, how he adjudicates, or what he prepares next?**

This is a stronger test than extracting stated philosophy. A case only qualifies here when there is evidence on more than one side of the decision: prior structure or policy, a live signal, and a later consequence or changed behavior.

## Cross-case distillation: when live play earns the right to change prep

**Repeated attention is evidence, not noise.** One mention does not automatically become a quest. Victor becomes prep-worthy because players research him, write to him, deliver the letter, discuss him again, and organize around him. Salience is accumulated behavior.

**Change prep at the smallest level that solves the actual problem.** A character-memory request is narrowed instead of erased; a boss fight becomes a smaller escort encounter; a delayed fight is moved rather than deleted. Brendon often preserves the useful core while changing delivery.

**Escalate the size of the rewrite when the failure is architectural.** When four-faction design collapses into a harmful two-faction winner-take-all system, a scene-level patch is insufficient. The ending structure changes, and dependent mechanics are removed.

**Player history progressively outbids speculative prep.** Early Season 3 is densely scheduled. By Week 5, accumulated goals and unfinished business are valuable enough that Brendon deliberately leaves authored space open and solicits player priorities.

**Consequences are more reusable than scripts.** Billie's death becomes Victor access; the Lake of Woes war changes Roostloft; a memory spell becomes a recurring 'look away' motif. After an event resolves, its consequences become future prep material.

**Table-legibility is part of fairness.** When danger lands wrong, Brendon does not simply erase danger. He identifies missing telegraphing and later makes lethal stakes explicit. Likewise, PvP consent determines whether a roll exists at all.

**The DM's job is to preserve the experience a piece of prep was for.** A prepared fight is disposable if the current party cannot experience it as intended. The activity can be replaced while its intended function—danger, action, continuity, payoff—survives.

**Restraint is an active intervention.** In the Victor case, promoting an NPC requires extra DM work; once players disagree about what to do with him, the correct move becomes less authorship. Brendon adds world, access, and adjudication, then deliberately stops choosing.

## Provisional salience-to-prep-mutation model

A useful model emerging from these cases is:

1. **Observe recurrence.** Has a player concern returned through action, conversation, resource spending, relationship, or risk?
2. **Check story-bearing capacity.** Does following it create a real decision, consequence, relationship, or payoff, rather than merely more content?
3. **Compare against current prep.** Does the live signal now matter more than the speculative material occupying that prep space?
4. **Choose mutation scale.** Adjust a ruling, scene, encounter, schedule, through-line, or campaign architecture at the smallest level that actually fixes the mismatch.
5. **Preserve causal residue.** Do not reset the world after the change. Carry consequences, motifs, relationships, losses, and altered faction states forward.
6. **Re-check ownership.** If the resulting situation is now primarily a player conflict or player-authored problem, stop steering its conclusion.
7. **Audit dependents.** If the change was structural, remove or rewrite downstream mechanics whose meaning depended on the old structure.

This is still a hypothesis, but unlike a personality statement it is supported by repeated observed transformations in live play.
